Website Evaluations

Apple (Good Design)

  • Contrast: Apple effectively uses high contrast with bold dark typography against clean white and neutral gray backgrounds. Key product images and primary call-to-action buttons pop distinctly, immediately drawing user attention to primary elements.
  • Repetition: The design exhibits visual consistency through repeated use of the San Francisco sans-serif typography, uniform product grid layouts, consistent icon styles, and a cohesive minimalist color scheme throughout every page.
  • Alignment: Every layout element adheres to a precise grid structure. Text, headings, and product images are aligned cleanly with clear vertical and horizontal anchor lines, creating a sleek and structured flow.
  • Proximity: Related elements such as product titles, taglines, pricing, and purchase links are grouped tightly together. Generous padding and white space clearly delineate distinct product sections and prevent visual clutter.

LINGsCars (Bad Design)

  • Contrast: The site exhibits severe contrast issues, featuring harsh neon text over busy animated backgrounds and patterned wallpapers. Low-contrast color pairings render critical text extremely difficult to read.
  • Repetition: There is almost no visual repetition or design system. The page uses dozens of mismatched font families, inconsistent text sizes, random animated GIFs, and competing button styles with no unifying structure.
  • Alignment: Elements lack grid alignment, appearing scattered across the screen. Floating widgets, misaligned banners, and overlapping text frames create visual chaos that disrupts reading patterns.
  • Proximity: Related items like car details, pricing, and lease terms are separated by unrelated banners, floating popups, and chaotic graphics. The lack of structured spacing makes it difficult to parse information logical groupings.
